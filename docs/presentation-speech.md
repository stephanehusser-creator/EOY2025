## INTRODUCTION & SLIDE 1: Title Slide

**SPEECH (Duration: ~2 minutes)**

Good morning, everyone. Thank you for joining me today.

As we close out 2025, I wanted to give you a comprehensive view of where we stand in the EMEA aviation market and, more importantly, where our opportunities lie for the year ahead.

This has been a transformative year for aviation in our region. We've seen the complete recovery from the pandemic – actually, we've exceeded pre-pandemic levels across all our territories. But more significantly, we've witnessed a fundamental shift in where growth is happening and what that means for our business strategy.

Today, I'll walk you through three key areas: First, the current state of the EMEA aviation market – and I'll show you why Europe and the Middle East are telling two very different stories. Second, where the real opportunities are for TAPIS, both in new aircraft deliveries and the retrofit market. And third, our specific targets for 2026 and the strategic accounts we need to win.

What you'll see is that the Middle East isn't just growing – it's growing three times faster than Europe. Dubai is now the world's number one international airport. Premium cabins are becoming standard even on low-cost carriers in the Gulf. And the wealth migration we're seeing – nearly ten thousand high-net-worth individuals moving to the UAE alone in 2024 – is fundamentally reshaping where premium travel demand exists.

But let me start with where we've traditionally been strongest: Europe. Because understanding the constraints there will help explain why we're pivoting our strategy.

## SLIDE 2: State of EMEA Aviation - Europe
**[SLIDE 2: European Market Overview]**

**SPEECH (Duration: ~4-5 minutes)**

Let's talk about Europe. And I want to be frank with you about what we're seeing.

On the surface, the numbers look good. Passenger traffic has recovered to 105% of 2019 levels. We're at 1.15 billion passengers across continental Europe this year. Domestic travel has stabilized, leisure traffic is actually up, and load factors are strong – 80 to 85 percent on most routes.

But here's the reality: this is a market in recovery mode, not growth mode.

Let me explain what I mean by walking through the critical challenges – because they directly impact our business.

**First, the workforce crisis.** Europe is short 18 to 20 thousand pilots right now. They're missing 30% of their aircraft engineers. And this isn't a temporary blip – the training infrastructure simply cannot close this gap for two to three years. Airlines are literally grounding aircraft they own because they don't have crews to fly them. When you can't staff your existing fleet, you certainly can't expand it aggressively.

**Second, sustainability costs are hitting hard.** The EU's ReFuelEU mandate and the Emissions Trading System are adding between 4 and 8 euros per passenger in costs. Airlines are responding by accelerating fleet renewal – replacing older aircraft with more efficient models – but they're not adding capacity. They're replacing one-for-one. For us, that means the 185 aircraft being delivered to Europe in 2026 are split almost evenly: 100 for growth, 85 for replacement.

**Third, the geopolitical situation.** Let me show you what's happening with China routes – this map illustrates the problem perfectly. [Reference the geopolitical map on Slide 4]. Russian airspace closure is forcing European carriers to add 2 to 4 hours to Asia flights. The result? Virgin Atlantic, SAS, and LOT have completely exited China routes. Finnair cut their China service by 90%. British Airways, Lufthansa, KLM – all down 20% or more. Chinese carriers now have 78% market share on Europe-China routes because they can still overfly Russia.

This matters to us because long-haul routes are where premium cabin demand lives. And Europe is losing long-haul reach.

**Fourth, infrastructure constraints.** Heathrow is at 98 to 99% capacity – there's literally no room to grow. Schiphol in Amsterdam has been forced to reduce flights. Air traffic control shortages, ground handling shortages – it's constraint after constraint.

Now, there ARE bright spots. Tourism to Spain, Greece, Turkey, and Italy is above 2019 levels. And we're seeing something interesting: European carriers are starting to put business class on single-aisle aircraft. Iberia just launched the world's first A321XLR with lie-flat business seats. Turkish Airlines ordered 220 A321neos with full business class. Eurowings is launching premium BIZ in November. So premium is creeping into the narrowbody fleet, which is good for us.

But the overall assessment? Europe is stable but constrained. It's efficiency-driven, not expansion-driven. Fleet growth is 2.4% – that's modest. And here's the key number: we're projecting 55 to 60 thousand yards of Ultraleather sales in EMEA for 2026. If Europe were our only market, we'd be looking at maybe 35% growth at best.

But Europe isn't our only market. In fact, it's not even our biggest opportunity anymore.

## SLIDE 3: State of EMEA Aviation - Europe Fleet Growth
**[SLIDE 3: Europe Fleet Growth Table]**

**SPEECH (Duration: ~2 minutes)**

Before we move to the Middle East, I want you to see these numbers in context.

[Point to the table] 4,200 aircraft in 2025, growing to 4,300 in 2026. That's 100 aircraft net growth – a 2.4% increase. Deliveries are steady at 185 aircraft each year.

Look at the composition: Single-aisle growing from 3,100 to 3,175 – that's 75 aircraft. Twin-aisle growing from 1,100 to 1,125 – that's 25 aircraft. So 74% of the European fleet is single-aisle, focused on short and medium-haul routes.

Now look at the carrier types. Legacy carriers growing 3.2%. Low-cost carriers growing 4.3%. The LCCs are taking market share, and they're the ones driving the narrowbody business class trend I mentioned.

And that orderbook: 850+ aircraft through 2035. So there's a pipeline. It's just spread over a decade.

Keep these numbers in your head – 2.4% growth, 185 deliveries, heavy single-aisle. Because when we look at the Middle East in a moment, you're going to see a completely different picture.

But first, let's acknowledge the elephant in the room: geopolitical risk.
